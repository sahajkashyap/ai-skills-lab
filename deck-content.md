## Slide 2 — What is AI?
AI stands for "Artificial Intelligence." That's a big name for a simple idea:
AI is a computer helper. You can talk to it or type to it. It can answer back.
You may have heard of these AI helpers: ChatGPT · Copilot · Gemini · Claude

## Slide 3 — How Does AI Learn?
AI learned by reading. A lot. It read everything. Books, websites, and
stories — more than any person could read in a hundred lives.

## Slide 4 — Here's the Tricky Part…
AI does not really "know" things the way you do. It just gets very good at
guessing the next word. It is like a super smart guess machine.

>> LAB: slides 2–4 compress into the lab's short intro (~150 words). "Super
>> smart guess machine" is the keeper line — it's the whole mental model.

## Slide 5 — What Can AI Do Well? (the five)
1. Write things · 2. Help you think · 3. Sum it up · 4. Answer questions ·
5. Help you plan

## Slide 6 — Thing 1: Write things
AI can write for you. Emails, notes, lists, even fixing your spelling.
TRY ASKING: "Write me a short, friendly email asking for a meeting next week."

>> LAB — EXERCISE 1 (DO): draft a real email you actually need to send this
>> week. Then the push: "make it sound like me — shorter, less formal."
>> CHECK-IN: does it still sound like you? What did you have to change?
>> (This plants the AI-drafts-you-fix habit from slide 19.)

## Slide 7 — Thing 2: Help you think
AI can give you lots of ideas, fast. Use it like a brainstorm partner.
TRY ASKING: "Give me 10 ideas for a fun work party."

>> LAB — EXERCISE 2 (DO): brainstorm 10 ideas for a real thing on their
>> plate; pick the best 2; then ask the AI to argue for each pick.
>> CHECK-IN: could you defend those 2 picks to a coworker in one sentence?
>> (Plants AI-suggests-you-pick, slide 18.)

## Slide 8 — Thing 3: Sum it up
AI can take a long story and tell you what it's about — in just a few words.
TRY ASKING: "Sum this up in 3 sentences." (then paste a long email)

>> LAB — EXERCISE 3 (DO): paste a real long email or article they've already
>> read, ask for 3 sentences.
>> CHECK-IN: did the summary drop anything YOU would have kept? That gap is
>> the lesson — the AI doesn't know what matters to you (slide 15's point,
>> taught by experience instead of assertion).

## Slide 9 — Thing 4: Answer questions
AI can answer simple and big questions. Just remember to check what it says.
TRY ASKING: "Why is the sky blue? Explain it like I'm 8."

>> LAB — EXERCISE 4 (DO): ask about something they already know cold — their
>> job, their hometown, their hobby — and grade the answer like a teacher:
>> right / vague / wrong, line by line.
>> CHECK-IN: where was it confidently vague? This is the warm-up for
>> Exercise 6.

## Slide 10 — Thing 5: Help you plan
Trips. Parties. School projects. Big jobs at work. AI can help you map it out.
TRY ASKING: "Make me a 3-day plan to clean out my office."

>> LAB — EXERCISE 5 (DO): turn a real upcoming task into a 3-step plan, then
>> find the step the AI got wrong or missed because it doesn't know their
>> situation. Fix it by telling the AI what it didn't know, and watch the
>> plan improve.
>> CHECK-IN: what did you have to tell it? (Slide 15's "it doesn't know
>> you," experienced firsthand.)

## Slide 12 — AI Can Make Stuff Up
NEW WORD: HALLUCINATION (hə-loo-sə-NAY-shun). It's when AI tells you
something that sounds true… but is not true at all. It might give you a fake
fact or a fake name. It does this with a smile, like it is sure. But it is
wrong.

>> LAB — EXERCISE 6, THE CENTERPIECE (DO): the learner deliberately hunts
>> for a confident wrong answer. Two reliable baits: (a) ask about something
>> that happened very recently; (b) ask for specific details about something
>> only they know well (their small company, their neighborhood, a niche
>> hobby). Catch it in the act.
>> CHECK-IN (the one that matters): write down the tell — how did
>> confident-and-wrong LOOK different from right? Answer: it didn't. That
>> sentence is the durable mindset the whole lab exists to install.
>> The pronunciation card stays — it's a signature teacher move.

## Slides 13–16 — Four Things to Watch For
1. Doesn't know what's new (learned from old text; double-check fresh facts)
2. Can't feel (sounds caring because it learned from people who care;
   feelings and tough talks stay human)
3. Doesn't know you (starting from zero every time; the more you share, the
   better it helps)
4. Not always right (smart-sounding ≠ correct; always check before you send,
   share, or save)

>> LAB: these four become the debrief AFTER Exercise 6 — by then the learner
>> has personally experienced #1 (recent-events bait), #3 (Exercises 3 & 5),
>> and #4 (Exercises 4 & 6). The lab points back at their own results:
>> "you already saw this happen — here's its name." #2 (can't feel) stays as
>> stated wisdom; no exercise should manufacture an emotional moment.

## Slide 17 — Turn & Talk
Your turn. When did AI not work the way you wanted? Or — when did it give you
a result that you knew (or felt) was off? Be ready to share with a partner.

>> LAB: becomes a written self-reflection box (self-paced has no partner):
>> same two questions, plus "what would you check before trusting a result
>> like that again?" In the facilitator deck it stays a Turn & Talk — one of
>> the visible live-vs-self-paced differences worth keeping.

## Slides 18–20 — How We Work With AI (the wrap)
Better together. AI + You = a great team.
- Way #1: AI gives ideas. You pick the good ones.
- Way #2: AI writes a draft. You fix it. (AI gets you started fast — then you
  make it sound like you.)
- Way #3: AI gives an answer. You check it. (Your eyes catch what AI misses.)

## What stays true in every conversion
- Plain language survives. No jargon enters. The reading level of the deck IS
  the product.
- Model-agnostic: every exercise works in Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot, or
  Gemini; Claude is the worked example (matching the Anthropic posting's
  "durable, model-agnostic mindsets").
- Every exercise uses the learner's REAL work — real email, real task, real
  expertise — never toy examples. That's the difference between a demo and a
  lab.
